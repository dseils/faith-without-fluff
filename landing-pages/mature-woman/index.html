An Invitation

For years, I did the right things. Said the right words. Showed up when I was supposed to.

And somewhere along the way, I realized I wasn't sure what I actually believed anymore—only what I was supposed to believe.

Watch the invitation

I served on committees. I taught Sunday school. I was there every time the doors opened.

But inside? I had questions I was afraid to voice. Doubts I thought meant I was failing. A longing for something real that I couldn't quite name.

Every time I tried to be honest, I felt like I was letting people down. Like faith was something to perform, not something to feel.

"The performance is exhausting. And it's not what we're called to."

You're not less faithful because you have questions. You're not less committed because you need something authentic. The problem isn't you—it's that too many spaces ask us to shrink ourselves to fit their mold.

You deserve a place where you can set down the performance.
